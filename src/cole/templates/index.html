Cost Of Living Explorer

Find a place that fits your life and your budget.

It’s easy to fall in love with a place before adding up what living there costs. COLE helps you consider the budget earlier.

Compare estimated household living costs across U.S. counties. Start with your take-home pay, adjust expenses you know, and narrow the search around what matters to you.

Before you enter anything

How COLE works

COLE compares your household's take-home pay with estimated living costs in each county. You can adjust the budget to see how much you would have left.

  1. 1
    Describe your household.

    Choose a supported household profile and enter your take-home pay after taxes.

  2. 2
    Set your priorities.

    Adjust known expenses, choose an optional reserve target, and apply the preferences that matter to you.

  3. 3
    Explore and compare.

    Inspect county estimates and compare up to three counties.

EPI’s starting budget estimates renter costs. Figures describe counties as a whole; check actual local costs before making a decision.

Budget rules and sources
  1. 1
    Start with your household and take-home pay.

    Choose the number of adults and children, then enter your household's take-home pay per month or year. Enter one amount to find counties with estimated costs up to that amount. Enter lower and upper amounts to search a cost range; a matching county can still cost more than your lower take-home pay. You can also look up a place with or without entering income.

  2. 2
    See estimated living costs.

    COLE starts with Economic Policy Institute (EPI) estimates for housing, food, transportation, health care, child care, and other necessities. We remove EPI's estimated taxes because your take-home pay is already after tax. EPI's housing estimate is for renters.

  3. 3
    Adjust the budget and leave room for your goals.

    Replace categories with costs you know, including your full monthly housing costs if you own. Add expenses the estimate doesn't cover. You can also choose a percentage of take-home to keep after costs. A 20% target on $90,000 leaves $18,000 for your goals and up to $72,000 for costs. An income range uses the lower amount for this target.

  4. 4
    Narrow the search, then compare your options.

    Filter by state, EPI cost categories, long-term temperatures, hazards, industry employment, poverty, educational attainment, age profiles and public outdoor space. Explore the county map and compare up to three counties. Each breakdown shows estimated costs, what remains at the income you entered, and whether you meet your reserve goal. You can change your criteria and search again.

What “remaining” means. Take-home pay minus estimated costs and your extra expenses. EPI does not include savings. Monthly budget figures are annual amounts divided by 12, and a positive balance alone doesn't tell you whether a place will feel comfortable. Your debts, savings goals, and unexpected expenses matter too.

Before you decide to move. These estimates describe a county as a whole. Prices for a particular home or neighborhood will vary. Your replacement costs apply to every county in a search. Check actual housing, bills, and local conditions before committing to a move.

Sources and privacy. Costs come from EPI's 2026 estimates in 2025 dollars; temperatures are NOAA's 1991 to 2020 monthly county averages. Census geography connects places to counties. Optional features use FEMA's December 2025 hazard data, BLS 2025 annual-average employment, and Census ACS 2020–2024 poverty and educational attainment and age estimates, plus USGS PAD-US 4.1 public outdoor space estimates. {% if hosted %}The free web edition processes your entries on COLE's server over HTTPS. COLE does not save financial entries; the hosting provider may retain connection and operational logs. {% else %}COLE runs on your computer and does not save your financial entries.{% endif %}

      Understand the estimate

      How the budget and filters are calculated

      COLE compares your household's take-home pay with EPI's renter estimates for each county. It removes EPI's estimated taxes because your take-home pay is already after tax, then adds your extra expenses. If you choose Your monthly costs, your monthly costs replace the EPI categories you fill in. Your income stays the same. Actual expenses can differ from these estimates.

      Cost and temperature filters narrow matching results without changing the source estimates. Cost limits use the original EPI monthly amounts shown in county details. Your extra expenses are added separately, without splitting them among categories. Seasonal temperature limits use the lowest and highest of NOAA's twelve monthly averages. The 12-month limits use COLE's average of those values, rounded to one decimal place. Without a reserve goal, a county can match your income range and still cost more than your lower take-home amount. Buffers widen the range of costs in your search. They never increase the money you have available.

      Looking up a city, place, or ZIP area finds the counties it overlaps. The cost and climate estimates describe those counties, not the city or postal ZIP itself.

      Household and housing assumptions

      EPI has estimates for one or two working adults and zero to four children. Two-adult EPI profiles assume married partners. For one, two, three, or four children, it assumes ages 4; 4 and 8; 4, 8, and 12; or 4, 8, 12, and 16. These ages affect food and child-care estimates; child ages also affect health-insurance premiums. COLE cannot recalculate the Published estimate for different ages.

      Housing basis: renter estimate. EPI uses local fair-market rent and tenant-paid utilities. Homeownership costs such as a mortgage, property tax, homeowners insurance, maintenance, and HOA fees are not estimated.

      Location lookup

      Choose a result below before submitting. Places and ZIP areas can cross county boundaries. ZIP areas are Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs); not every postal ZIP has a match. COLE reports their associated county estimates; it does not create a city-level or postal-ZIP estimate.

      Total household take-home pay

      Enter annual or monthly after-tax totals. COLE does not estimate take-home pay from gross salary or dollars per hour. If you are paid hourly or your pay varies, use your pay records to find an annual total or a typical monthly total after tax. You can also use a separate payroll calculator for the place you're considering. Enter an upper total, or lower and upper totals to search a cost range. Supply a lower amount for every earner, or leave every lower field blank. A typed 0 is a real lower income of $0.

      If your take-home pay is steady, multiply the amount deposited each payday by the number of payments in a year: weekly × 52, every two weeks × 26, twice monthly × 24, or monthly × 12. Adjust for unpaid time and known variation. COLE adds every earner into one household total.

      Use the take-home pay expected in the state being compared. If no state income tax applies to your household there, no extra COLE adjustment is needed. Each search uses the same take-home totals for every county. Run a separate search if a destination's taxes would substantially change your pay. COLE does not subtract taxes again. Health premiums, retirement contributions, and other payroll deductions may overlap with EPI cost categories. Do not add an expense again if it is already deducted from the take-home amount you enter. For replacement costs, enter what you still pay out of pocket.

      Without a reserve goal, a range searches total estimated costs between the lower and upper amounts. Cheaper counties need a lower-cost buffer to appear. A match can still cost more than your lower take-home pay.

      Your income is not saved. {% if hosted %}COLE sends it over HTTPS to its server to calculate this scenario. COLE does not save it to a user profile, browser storage or database. The hosting provider may retain connection and operational logs. {% else %}COLE uses it for this local session. It does not save these values in your browser or in a local data file.{% endif %}

      Location: state restriction

      When searching for matches, show only counties in the selected state. Leave this set to All supported states and DC to search the full supported area. Looking up a place can still show counties across state lines; counties outside your selected state are marked. When comparing states, enter the take-home pay you expect in each destination.

      Extra monthly expenses

      Add one monthly amount for expenses beyond the EPI household estimate. For example, $300 per month adds $3,600 to each county's annual cost. Blank or 0 adds nothing. If you use Your monthly costs, extras are added after your category replacements. Count each expense only once.

      Students should include tuition, required fees, books, course software, and other recurring costs not already covered by their budget. For semester bills, add every term you expect to pay for during the year, then divide by 12. Divide annual bills by 12 too. Do not add an expense again if it is already in a budget category or deducted from the take-home pay you entered.

      Published costs and category limits

      Food and child care are separate estimates, though both use EPI's assumed child ages. These published costs are in 2025 dollars:

      • Housing: rent and tenant-paid utilities, not homeownership.
      • Food: low-cost groceries mostly prepared at home, adjusted for local prices and the assumed household ages.
      • Transportation: estimated vehicle and transit costs under EPI's working-adult assumptions; not a measure of service or walkability.
      • Child care: full-time care for age 4, school-year and summer care for age 8, summer care for age 12, and none for age 16. Infant and toddler care is not included.
      • Health care: marketplace insurance premiums and out-of-pocket medical costs.
      • Other necessities: clothing, personal care, household supplies, phone service, reading, and school supplies.

      Each box sets the highest EPI category estimate you want in your matching results. It does not replace your personal spending. Blank means no limit; $0 requires a $0 EPI estimate for that category. Looking up a place still shows its counties and marks any limits they miss. Extra expenses are added separately.

      Read EPI's 2026 methods.

      Your monthly costs

      Replace a category only when you know your monthly out-of-pocket cost. Blank keeps the EPI amount; 0 replaces it with $0. Replace at least one category, or select Published estimate. COLE adjusts EPI's published renter total, with estimated taxes removed, by the difference between your cost and EPI's. You can still see the original EPI figures.

      • Housing: include rent plus utilities, or all monthly owner costs such as mortgage, tax, insurance, utilities, and maintenance. EPI's source is renter based. Include HOA and applicable mortgage/flood insurance; do not add escrowed taxes or insurance twice. Down payment, closing, and moving costs are separate upfront needs, not covered by this recurring monthly total.
      • Food: enter your household's food spending after any SNAP benefit. This is separate from child care.
      • Transportation: include the travel and vehicle costs you will actually pay, even if you do not commute.
      • Health care: include your premiums and out-of-pocket costs.
      • Other necessities: include clothing, personal care, phone, and household supplies.
      • Child care: enter your actual care cost, including $0 if no paid care is needed.

      CFPB: what a total mortgage payment includes (internet required).

      COLE uses these same costs in every county you search. It does not check whether you qualify for vouchers, SNAP, or insurance, or whether benefits and housing are available. Limit your search to places where you know a benefit applies. Extra expenses are added separately, so count each cost only once. EPI category limits still use the original EPI amounts.

      Reading long-term temperatures

      Enter degrees Fahrenheit (°F). Fill in the limits you care about and leave the rest blank. These are NOAA's monthly average temperatures for county areas from 1991 to 2020. They describe past conditions, not a forecast or a guarantee for a specific address.

      Coldest and warmest mean the lowest and highest of the twelve monthly averages, not average daily low or high temperatures. COLE averages those twelve values and rounds to one decimal place for the 12-month figure. A county must meet every cost and temperature limit to appear in matching results. Looking up a place still shows its counties and marks any missed limits or missing temperature data.

      Widen the cost search

      Buffers change which counties appear. They never increase your take-home pay. The lower-cost buffer searches below your minimum and is available only when every earner has a lower take-home amount. If all lower amounts are blank, clear any existing lower-cost buffer to $0. The higher-cost buffer can still include costs above your upper amount.

      Examples: Leave the lower amount blank and enter $50,000 as the upper amount to search qualifying estimates up to $50,000; a $3,000 yearly higher-cost buffer extends that ceiling to $53,000. For a $40,000 to $50,000 range, a $2,000 yearly lower buffer and $3,000 yearly higher buffer search estimated costs from $38,000 to $53,000.

      How EPI builds the estimate

      EPI estimates a modest but adequate renter budget for each county and supported household type. It covers essentials. Your actual spending may differ, and the estimate does not tell you whether a particular home is available.

      • Housing: HUD fair-market rent and tenant-paid utilities.
      • Food: USDA low-cost groceries, adjusted for local food prices.
      • Transportation: modeled car ownership, car use, and transit costs for working adults.
      • Child care: child-care price data with EPI's assumed child ages and care schedules.
      • Health care: marketplace insurance premiums and out-of-pocket costs.
      • Other necessities: an allowance based on household spending data.

      EPI also estimates taxes. COLE removes that amount from EPI's published total before comparing the remaining costs with your after-tax pay. EPI provides 10 family types and no 3-adult estimate. Read EPI's 2026 methods.

      Your reserve goal

      Choose the percentage of take-home you want to keep after estimated costs and your extra expenses. A 20% goal on $90,000 keeps $18,000 and leaves up to $72,000 for costs.

      If you enter an income range, COLE checks the goal against your lower take-home amount. Cheaper counties can still match. Widened cost searching stays off until you clear the goal. You won't need to enter their amounts again.

      CFPB describes a flexible 50/30/20 budgeting guideline, including 20% for savings goals. COLE checks how much is left after costs against the percentage you choose. It does not check the full guideline or guarantee a comfortable budget. Count savings only once if they're already deducted from your paycheck or included in extras. Read CFPB guidance (internet required).

      What belongs in housing?

      If you rent, include rent and the utilities you pay. If you own, include mortgage principal and interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, any flood or mortgage insurance, HOA fees, utilities, and money for maintenance. Count taxes and insurance only once if your mortgage payment already includes them through escrow. A paid-off home still has other costs. Phone and internet may already be in Other necessities.

      Your monthly total replaces EPI's renter housing estimate in every county you search. Run a separate search to try another property's costs. Down payment, closing costs, and moving expenses are separate upfront needs. Leave the field blank to keep EPI.

      Reading your budget results

      Remaining is take-home pay minus the selected cost estimate and extras. A shortfall means those costs exceed that income. Monthly figures are annual totals divided by 12.

      If you set a reserve goal, the amount remaining still includes the money you want to keep. The goal line compares that remainder with your target and shows how far above or below it you are. Don't subtract that difference from the remaining amount again.

      EPI does not include savings. Having a little money left may still leave you short on emergencies, debt payments, or personal goals. Account for those needs, and count savings only once if they're already deducted from pay or entered in extras.

      Without a reserve goal, a county can match your income range and still cost more than your lower income. Costs-only lookups do not calculate remaining money.
